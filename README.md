# 2025 Tariffs and Data Center Impact

**Project Overview**
This project examines how tariffs on construction materials could impact the growing U.S. data center industry. The analysis focuses on the potential consequences of these policies for critical AI infrastructure development, visualizing data on global data center distribution, projected energy demands, material sourcing dynamics, and big tech investment plans.

**Tools & Packages Used**

- Adobe Illustrator for creating data visualizations
- HTML & CSS for responsive web presentation
- Datawrapper for interactive charts and ai2html for creating responsive visualizations from Illustrator files
- SVG for scalable graphics

**Data Collection**

Global Data Center Distribution Data
Data on the global distribution of data centers was collected from industry reports including CBRE's North America Data Center Trends H2 2024 and Brightlio's data center statistics. These sources highlight the U.S. dominance in this sector with significantly more facilities than other countries.
Energy Consumption Projections
Current and forecasted energy consumption data for U.S. data centers was compiled from Department of Energy reports and Mordor Intelligence's U.S. Data Center Market analysis, showing the dramatic expected growth from current 2% of U.S. electricity consumption to a projected 8% by 2030.
Material Sourcing Analysis
Information on material sourcing for data center construction was gathered through industry publications including ComputerWeekly's analysis on tariff impacts on data center equipment costs and DataCenterDynamics' coverage of Trump's tariffs.
Big Tech Investment Plans
Investment figures were collected from company financial reports and analyses from Reuters, Fortune, and Forbes regarding big tech's data center spending plans and how tariffs might affect them.

**Visualizations**

- Global Data Center Distribution Chart: A horizontal bar chart showing the United States' dominant position in global data center infrastructure compared to other countries. The visualization highlights that the U.S. operates more facilities than all other countries combined.
- Energy Demand Projection Chart: A line chart displaying the current and projected energy demand from U.S. data centers through 2030, showing the dramatic expected increase in power consumption.
- Material Sourcing Diagram: An illustration showing the flow of materials (steel, aluminum, copper) for data center construction and how tariffs impact this supply chain.
- Big Tech Investment Comparison: A bar chart comparing planned investments in data center infrastructure from major tech companies, visualizing the scale of capital commitments.

**Key Findings**

The United States operates substantially more data centers than any other country, maintaining a critical advantage in AI infrastructure
Energy demand from data centers is projected to quadruple by 2030, requiring massive infrastructure investment
Tariffs on steel, aluminum, and copper could significantly increase construction costs for new data centers
Major tech companies have announced hundreds of billions in planned investments that could be disrupted by policy uncertainty
These tariffs create a policy contradiction between promoting domestic manufacturing and maintaining U.S. leadership in AI infrastructure

**Future Development & Research**

Next steps for this project could explore:
